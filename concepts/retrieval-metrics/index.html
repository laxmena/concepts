Precision, recall and reciprocal rank

These three scores grade a search result list when each result is simply relevant or not. They are the usual first checks for search and for the retrieval step of RAG.

  • Precision@k asks how many of the top k results are relevant.
  • Recall@k asks how many of all the relevant pages made it into the top k.
  • Reciprocal rank asks how early the first relevant page appears.
  • Each score ignores something the others see.

Show the formulas
precision@k = (relevant results in the top k) / k recall@k = (relevant results in the top k) / R reciprocal rank = 1 / (rank of the first relevant result), or 0 if none MRR = average of reciprocal rank over all queries

Example for MRR: three queries whose first correct answers come at ranks 3, 2 and 1 give (1/3 + 1/2 + 1) / 3 = 0.61.

Where you meet it

A help-centre search returns ten pages. Support wants to know whether the right page is near the top, and whether a customer who reads the first five has seen everything useful.

  • Precision@k suits a screen that shows only k results, such as the top five on a phone.
  • Recall@k suits a retrieval step that feeds a later step, such as the passages a RAG system hands to a language model: a page that is not retrieved cannot be used.
  • Reciprocal rank suits a question with one right answer, such as a lookup or a navigation search.

Where it breaks

Precision@k assumes there are at least k relevant pages. When the collection holds fewer, even a perfect system cannot score 1. Pick how many relevant pages exist, and look at a perfect list.

With one relevant page, a perfect list scores on precision@10. With three, . The system is perfect in both cases, so compare precision only between lists of the same query, or use a score that knows the best case, like nDCG.

Check yourself

A list has 3 relevant pages in the top 10, out of 12 in the collection. What are precision@10 and recall@10?

Precision@10 = 3/10 = 0.3. Recall@10 = 3/12 = 0.25.

You swap two relevant pages that are both inside the top k. Which of the three scores can change?

Only reciprocal rank, and only if one of them was first. Precision@k and recall@k count what is inside the top k and ignore the order.

Three queries have their first relevant result at ranks 1, 2 and 4. What is the MRR?

(1 + 1/2 + 1/4) / 3 = 1.75 / 3 = 0.58.
