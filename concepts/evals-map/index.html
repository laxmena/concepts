The map of evals

An eval is a test for an AI system. This is the map of the series: which eval fits which question.

  • First decide what you are grading: a search step, an answer, or an agent’s whole run.
  • Then decide who grades: a fixed rule, a program, a language model, people, or real users.
  • Every eval has a blind spot, shown live on its page.
  • Whatever you pick, ask how far to trust the number.

Where you meet it

A team ships a support bot. They check retrieval with recall@k, grounding with a RAG eval, wording with a judge model, and the whole thing with an A/B test. No one of these tells them the bot is good.

  • Most real eval suites combine a cheap offline check, such as a rule, a program or a judge model, with a slower online check on real users.
  • Each page here quotes the paper or reference it is checked against, so you can read further.

Check yourself

You have a code model and a test suite for each problem. Which eval fits, and what should you watch for?

pass@k, which runs the tests. Watch the tests themselves: weak tests let wrong code pass, so the score can rise without the model improving.

You want to compare two chat models on open questions, with no reference answers. What are your options?

Pairwise votes from people or a judge model, ranked with Elo or Bradley-Terry. Check a judge model for position and length bias, and check people for agreement with kappa.

Your offline eval says a change helped by 2 points on 300 questions. What do you do before you ship?

Ask whether 2 points is more than noise with 300 questions (it probably is not), then confirm on real users with an A/B test sized in advance.
