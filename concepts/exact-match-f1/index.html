Exact match and F1

Exact match and F1 compare a model’s answer to a reference answer. The SQuAD reading benchmark scores answers this way.

  • Exact match gives 1 when the answer equals the reference after cleaning, and 0 otherwise.
  • Cleaning means lowercase, no punctuation, and no “a”, “an” or “the”.
  • F1 gives partial credit for the words the answer and the reference share.
  • Both compare words, not meaning.

Show the formula
precision = shared words / words in the answer recall = shared words / words in the reference F1 = 2 × precision × recall / (precision + recall)

A word counts as shared as many times as it appears in both texts, so repeating a word does not help. F1 is the harmonic mean, which stays low if either precision or recall is low.

Where you meet it

A question-answering team compares two models on a test of short factual questions. One answers in a few words. The other answers in full sentences. The short one wins on exact match, and the sentence writer loses, even when its sentences are right.

  • The SQuAD paper (2016) has more than 100,000 questions on Wikipedia articles, and reports 51.0% F1 for a baseline model against a human score of 86.8%.
  • BLEU (2002) and ROUGE (2004) are the same kind of check. BLEU was made for machine translation and ROUGE for summaries. Both count shared word groups with a reference text.
  • The Codex paper tested whether BLEU tracks working code. It found that programs that are not equivalent to the reference often had higher BLEU scores than programs that are, so it scored code by running unit tests instead.

Where it breaks

These metrics count words, so they cannot see meaning. A right answer in other words scores 0. A wrong answer that reuses the right words scores high. Each card shows the score next to what a person would say.

Check yourself

The reference is “the Eiffel Tower”. The answer is “Eiffel Tower!”. What are exact match and F1?

Both are 1. Cleaning removes the “the” and the “!”, so both texts become “eiffel tower”.

An answer has 10 words and contains the 2-word reference. What is its F1?

Shared words: 2. Precision 2/10 = 0.2, recall 2/2 = 1. F1 = 2 × 0.2 × 1 / 1.2 = 0.33. Long answers are punished even when they contain the right words.

Why might a team still use exact match when F1 gives partial credit?

When the answer is a short fixed thing, like a date or an ID, partial credit means nothing. “1813” and “1831” share no word, but a near miss is still wrong.
